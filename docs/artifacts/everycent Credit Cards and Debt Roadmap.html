00

Foundation already in place

Done

Phase 7 starts with real credit and loan accounts, linked payment categories, transfers, split transactions, exact budget-month history and server-side money math. Do not rebuild these foundations.

Built

  • Credit card and line-of-credit account types; loan account families and stored loan terms P3
  • One managed payment category per credit account, linked by linkedAccountId / paymentCategoryId P4
  • Linked transfer pairs, split transfers, starting balances, account close/reopen and register undo P5–6
  • Assigned, Activity, Available and Ready to Assign calculated from transaction and assignment history P6

Known gaps entering Phase 7

  • Every overspent category is currently treated as cash overspending
  • Payment categories behave like ordinary categories (Credit Spending is real since 7a)
  • Loan terms are stored but do not affect transactions or projections
  • Credit-card close adjustments and cross-family account type changes need debt-aware rules
7a

Credit-aware budget math

Done

Separate cash spending from credit spending before moving any money. Every later card rule needs an exact answer to how much category activity was paid with cash, with a card, or by a mix of both.

Includes

  • Classify transaction and split-line activity by account family without changing total Activity
  • Return real Cash Spending and Credit Spending in category/month breakdowns
  • Engine primitives for funded credit activity, credit overspending and card balance by month: moved to 7b/7c, because the funded-first ordering is still a 7c verification gate
  • Keep uncategorized card spending and future-dated card transactions in the same cutoff rules as Phase 6
  • Build table-driven engine tests first: cash only, card only, mixed accounts, splits, returns and multiple cards

Depends on

  • Phase 6 month history and category inspector

Acceptance

  • The category's Activity is unchanged; Cash Spending + Credit Spending explains its outflows
  • No money moves to a payment category yet—this stage changes observation, not behavior
  • All amounts remain bigint minor units; classification happens on the server

Found in YNAB (web app, 3 Oct 2026)

  • Cash Spending and Credit Spending are each the net of the month's activity in that account family, refunds included: Wallet −$4 and +$10 showed Cash Spending $6.00 (positive); Visa −$5, +$2, a −$7 split line and Amex −$3 showed Credit Spending −$13.00
  • A split line is classified by its transaction's account; a +$1 Groceries line on Visa showed Credit Spending $1.00
  • Uncategorized splits the same way (Cash −$2.00, Credit −$1.00); its "will be deducted" note quoted only the cash part ($2.00), which is 7c credit overspending
  • The category Activity popover lists each transaction with its account (Wallet, Visa, Amex); a payment category's popover is a different breakdown (Spending, Returns, Funded Spending, Payments & Returns) — evidence for 7b in docs/verify.md
7b

Funded card spending and payment categories

Done

A funded card purchase spends a category's cash while reserving the same amount for the card payment. This is the core credit-card loop and must be derived from history, not maintained by fragile side-effect rows.

Includes

  • Spending on a credit account moves the funded portion into that account's linked payment category P7
  • Payment-category Available = direct Assigned + carried payment money + funded card spending − payments/refunds that release it
  • Credit Card Payments group subtotals and each card's Available pill
  • Edits, deletes, category changes, splits and undo/redo recompute the reservation correctly
  • Several cards spending from one category reserve money independently for each card

Depends on

  • 7a credit activity classification

Confirmed behavior

  • YNAB moves money only for the funded part of a categorized purchase; cash is funded first, whatever the entry order; a card's spending and returns in a category net out
  • Provisional (in docs/verify.md): newest card funded first when money runs short; a return on one card can fund another card's spending
  • The spending category falls and the card's payment category rises by the same funded amount
  • A genuinely positive card balance behaves as cash; automatic movement resumes only after the balance becomes negative

Design constraint

  • Compute from immutable transaction/assignment history where possible; if persisted derived rows are required, update them atomically with the source edit and prove replay safety
7c

Credit overspending and existing debt

Done

Not every card purchase has cash behind it. The unfunded part becomes debt instead of reducing next month's Ready to Assign, while debt that existed before everycent is funded directly in the payment category.

Includes

  • Credit overspending shown yellow and excluded from next month's cash-overspending deduction P7
  • Mixed overspending: split the category shortfall between cash and credit using YNAB's confirmed ordering rule
  • Negative credit starting balance creates debt but no category activity or automatic payment money
  • Assigning directly to the payment category reserves cash to pay existing debt
  • Payment-category funded/underfunded comparison against the amount owed, without implementing Phase 8 targets

Depends on

  • 7b payment-category math

Confirmed behavior

  • If a spending category has no cash, YNAB cannot move payment money; the card balance grows and the payment category does not
  • Old card debt is funded by assigning money directly to the card's payment category
  • Target-controlled debt payoff remains Phase 8

Found in YNAB (web app, 3 Oct 2026)

  • Mixed shortfall is cash first: the uncovered cash is cash overspending (red, deducted next month), the rest credit (yellow, new debt)
  • Covering credit overspending in its own month, even a past one, moves the money to the payment category; it never rolls over
  • Payment category: yellow while Available is below what's owed; inspector shows Total Underfunded = Overspent Categories + Underfunded
  • Still open: wording when both apply, what "Cover Overspent Categories" does, past-month colour (docs/verify.md)
7d

Payments, returns and refunds

Done

Transfers and inflows close the loop: paying the card consumes reserved payment money, while a return unwinds the original spending category and its card reservation.

Includes

  • A transfer from a budget cash account to a credit account reduces payment-category Available P7
  • Payments reduce the card balance and cash account balance without ordinary category Activity
  • Returns categorized to the original spending category reverse card activity and payment money
  • Statement credits / inflows to Ready to Assign follow YNAB's distinct behavior
  • Overpayment makes the payment category red when more was paid than Available
  • Split payments, partial refunds, deleted transfer sides and undo/redo stay consistent

Depends on

  • 7b funded reservations; 7c debt state

Confirmed behavior

  • Card payments are account transfers and carry no category
  • A refund should normally use the purchase category so money returns there and the amount reserved for payment is reduced
  • A positive balance from overpayment enters the plan differently from a categorized refund

Found in YNAB (web app, 3 Oct 2026)

  • A payment from a budget account takes its amount out of the payment category in its month; overpaying turns it red and is cash overspending (deducted next month)
  • Statement credits to Ready to Assign and cash advances leave the payment category alone
  • Still open: refunds in a later month or larger than the purchase, payments from tracking accounts (docs/verify.md)

7f

Loan activity and amortization

Deferred

Loan accounts need a purpose-built activity model: a payment is principal, interest and optional escrow/fees, with the loan balance changing only by principal and capitalized activity.

Includes

  • Loan payment transfer flow with principal, interest, escrow and extra payment breakdown P7
  • Monthly interest estimate from exact decimal rate math—never binary floats
  • First payment in a month applies interest and escrow/fees before principal
  • Add/edit manual interest and escrow activity for current and past months
  • Account activity timeline and current principal balance
  • Multiple and partial payments in one month, edits, deletes and undo/redo

Depends on

  • Existing loan types and terms; transaction transfer foundation

Confirmed behavior

  • YNAB models standard amortization with interest compounded monthly
  • Estimated monthly interest = current balance × annual rate ÷ 12, then the first payment of the month covers interest (and escrow/fees) before principal
  • Interest does not accrue automatically without a payment; the user can add it manually
  • Users can edit interest and escrow activity to match the lender

Verification gate

  • Exact rounding at the minor unit, effective balance/date used for a mid-month first payment, and negative-amortization behavior
  • How linked plan categories and payees are created for existing vs new loans
7g

Loan planner and finish Phase 7

Deferred

Once recorded loan activity is trustworthy, project the payoff: show what the current payment costs in time and interest, then let the user compare extra-payment scenarios without mutating the ledger.

Includes

  • Projected payoff month and total remaining interest from current balance, rate and payment
  • What-if extra monthly payment: new payoff date, months saved and interest saved P7
  • Escrow excluded from debt reduction; minimum payment and extra principal modeled separately
  • Impossible-plan states: zero/too-small payment, zero rate, already paid off and missing loan terms
  • Pure engine amortization schedule with deterministic tests; compact account-page planner UI

Depends on

  • 7f loan balance and activity semantics

Acceptance

  • Projection never writes transactions or assignments
  • Displayed totals exactly equal the generated schedule's principal and interest sums
  • Known amortization examples and YNAB test-account scenarios agree to the minor unit after confirmed rounding
  • pnpm check passes; OpenAPI/client and any migrations are generated and committed

Not here

  • Debt-payment targets and target progress (Phase 8)
  • Scheduled recurring loan payments (Phase 10)
  • Daily-variable or foreign-currency loan models

Ground rules

  • Match YNAB one to one; check, don't assume. Reproduce each verification gate in the signed-in YNAB test plan before implementing it. If it cannot be settled, add the exact question and evidence to docs/verify.md and skip that behavior.
  • Money and rates stay exact. Currency uses bigint minor units. Interest rates need a fixed-scale integer/rational representation; never Number(amount), parseFloat or floating-point amortization.
  • The server owns derived debt math. Clients render payment Available, funded credit activity, principal and projections returned by tested engine/API code.
  • History must replay. Editing, deleting, splitting, transferring, undoing and redoing a transaction must derive the same card/loan state as entering the final history from scratch.
  • Build one vertical stage at a time. Engine rule → PostgreSQL integration test → API schema → generated client → UI → browser parity check. Run pnpm check before marking a stage done.
